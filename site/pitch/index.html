What the site says today

Every page funnels to one exit, "Book a Free Call", under seven different labels, with no pricing, no eligibility check and no forwardable proof for a board. The proof exists but is scattered: 780+ clients (the homepage also says 750+), $48M+ managed, a written acquisition guarantee, one of nine inaugural Google-certified agencies, and a direct endorsement from Google's first Head of Ad Grants. Underneath: 618 undated blog posts, 54 case studies with placeholder testimonials, seven service pages on one broken template, and a Webflow staging domain leaking into live links.

Who is buying

Executive directors and communications directors at small and mid-size nonprofits, most of them women, most of them tired: 46% of nonprofit CEOs call burnout a serious concern in 2026. Their concrete fear is losing the grant. Google deactivates accounts that miss a 5% click-through floor two months running, and suspends without notice for policy violations. They research before they call, sell the decision to a board, and reward calm competence over hype. "Loud" is the current site's register and the wrong one.

Where the white space is

The electric-blue SaaS ground is the most crowded territory in the category: Nonprofit Megaphone shares it with Funraise, Whole Whale and Getting Attention, and is the loudest of the four. Navy-plus-orange is crowded too, and green is filling fast. Nobody owns proof rendered as a financial document, warm institutional editorial, vermilion as the megaphone's signal color, or real cause photography set with rigorous type. Each direction below claims one of those territories. One keeps the blue on purpose, so the choice between evolution and rupture is made deliberately.

Amplitude

The megaphone made literal and accountable: brand blue at the origin, Google's 5% floor as the innermost dashed ring, and three dated client outcomes pinned to the outer arcs, so the first screen is an input/output record on warm paper with one vermilion signal instead of a claim on a blue wall.

The idea

Nonprofit Megaphone's name promises amplification, and the current site says 'loud' the only way the category knows how: a saturated blue field. Amplitude replaces the blue with the physics of the metaphor, then makes the physics accountable. Every key surface is built from one diagram: an origin (the brand's own #0A23A2 dot, with a named account lead holding a $10,000/month grant beside it), rays leaving it up-and-right in the direction the existing mark already points, seven concentric arcs that gain weight as they travel, and level bars that rise at each arc's crossing of the output axis. The innermost ring is dashed and labelled 'Google's 5% CTR floor', so policy literacy is visible before the first scroll. Three real outcomes (COPD Foundation 300+ screenings/month, UNCF $38,000/month in donations, Solar Sister +3,000% search traffic) are pinned exactly where a ray meets an arc, each with vertical, twelve-month period and a case-file link, so a board-forwarding ED reads a record, not a promise. Real sound attenuates; on this site the signal grows, which is precisely NPM's claim, and every chip proves it with a date.

The diagram is declared in two layers. The geometry (rays, arcs, bars, trace, origin ring, ruled grid) is decorative and lives in one aria-hidden inline SVG sized to its grid column. Everything that carries information (the floor label, the lead chip, the three outcome chips, the axis label) is real DOM text, and a visually hidden figcaption states the whole argument in one sentence. That split is also what lets the hero degrade: when the hero container drops below 960px the arc field is swapped for the stacked level bar, the same component the phone hero uses, and the h1 scales with container units so it always holds two lines. Motion is pure CSS (arcs grow from the origin, bars rise, chips fade up, 1.4s total) and collapses to the final static state under prefers-reduced-motion; no JavaScript is required for any of it.

The panel's sharpest critiques were that trust was carried by geometry rather than evidence and that the field had no human in it. Both are fixed at the source: the account lead sits at the origin as a person, Amber Snow's portrait and 'Our guarantee is a person, not a policy' sit under the primary CTA, and the trust ledger is a framed Letter from Google (Michelle Hurtado at 21px serif), a status ticker with an 'as of' date, and a typographic client roll set in five voices between hairlines like a donor wall. The register stays confident calm with a pulse: warm paper, near-black ink, one vermilion for arcs and the single emphasised headline word, a darker vermilion for buttons and small labels so every piece of text clears AA, and the mark's blue kept exactly where the brand lives: the logo tile, the origin dot, the Google stamp and the focus ring.

What sets it apart. It is the only direction whose signature is generated from the brand's own mark (the up-and-right vector rendered as rays, arcs and level bars on warm paper with a vermilion signal) rather than borrowed from a category like a newspaper, a civic form, a signed letter or the current blue wall.

The ground is re-set so the direction is no longer a cream-and-red sibling of Amplitude. Risks

  • The note is an operations promise: an invented or stale sentence is the one thing this buyer punishes. Mitigated by the CMS collection, owner-per-account and fallback state above, and by copy that says 'every monthly report', not a fixed weekday.
  • Photography that slips leaves the plate half-empty. The duotone frames are honest placeholders for the pitch, not for launch; every photo-led section has a typographic fallback (roster, ledger, labelled screenshots), and the hero can ship as note-only at 380px centred until the portrait exists.
  • Linen, green and a hand-drawn underline can decay into stationery in an editor's hands. Guardrails are tokens in the Webflow style guide: ochre in four uses only, one green band above the fold and one below, three paper devices per page (note, stamp, underline), Fraunces SOFT never above 20, 11px floor, 3px/5px/pill radii only.
  • Dropping blue is a real equity change for 780 clients and Google's partner directory. Mitigation: the mark keeps its geometry, the Google Partner badge keeps its own rules, and the change is announced as a sharpening with old and new lockups side by side.
  • Ochre is a low-contrast hue: the direction depends on the three-tier ochre rule (fill #C8912A, display #A8761A at 40px+, ink #86590E for small text). A build that uses raw ochre for links or captions fails AA immediately; the token sheet names the three and the style guide shows the failing case.
  • Two dark green surfaces plus duotone frames make the page heavier than the other directions; if the client wants the lightest possible register, keep the nav band and drop the guarantee band to a hairline section.

Recommendation

Amplitude

Amplitude is the only direction whose signature is generated from the brand's own mark rather than borrowed from a newspaper, a government form, a signed letter or the current blue wall, and in the finished files it is evidenced rather than merely drawn: a blue origin with a named lead, Google's 5% floor as the innermost dashed ring, and three dated, case-filed outcome chips make the first screen a sourced input/output record. It carries the full trust layer (dated ticker, Letter from Google in serif, the person-not-policy guarantee under the CTA) while the warm paper, .6-capped arcs and near-black ink keep it calm enough for a tired ED, and the 52px primary plus 48px eligibility button pair sits above the fold on both frames (phone CTA at 594px, eligibility at 656px). In a category where every competitor paints blue edge to edge it is the most distinctive of the five, yet it keeps #0A23A2 precisely where the equity lives: the mark tile, the origin, the Google stamp, the focus ring. Buildability is better than it looks: static-first inline SVG, CSS-only motion with a reduced-motion final state, no JavaScript dependency, and a container-query fallback to the same level bar the phone uses; the real costs are four Google families to subset and a locked token set that a Webflow editor cannot drift.

Runner-up

Broadsheet

Broadsheet is the lowest-risk, most forwardable direction and the one a leadership team will find easiest to approve, because it keeps the brand's deep blue as the page's only chromatic colour so the redesign reads to 780 clients as a sharpening rather than a rupture. Its 'On the record' ledger with the hatched bar labelled ILLUSTRATIVE, the CMS-driven dateline and the sourced deactivation footnote is the most honest evidence object in the set, which is exactly what a board-screening buyer rewards. It is also the cheapest to build well in Webflow: two Google fonts, no mono, rules instead of cards, one inline SVG chart, GSAP optional, and a phone hero that puts the CTA at roughly 420px. Its weakness is ownability: serif-on-white editorial is a wide lane, its arcs are borrowed from Amplitude, and the hero is the most type-dense of the five, so it wins on trust and buildability and loses on distinctiveness.

  • Scope beyond the homepage. 716 URLs: 618 blog posts without dates or bylines, 54 case studies, seven service pages, a 60-person team page. Whether this is a Webflow rebuild or a replatform, and who owns the CMS migration and redirect map, is a separate decision.

  • The booking flow. Seven CTA labels, a fear-led contact headline, an unstyled calendar embed and HubSpot form. Every direction needs its own treatment of these.
