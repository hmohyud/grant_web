The idea

Nonprofit Megaphone's name promises amplification, and the current site says 'loud' the only way the category knows how: a saturated blue field. Amplitude replaces the blue with the physics of the metaphor, then makes the physics accountable. Every key surface is built from one diagram: an origin (the brand's own #0A23A2 dot, with a named account lead holding a $10,000/month grant beside it), rays leaving it up-and-right in the direction the existing mark already points, seven concentric arcs that gain weight as they travel, and level bars that rise at each arc's crossing of the output axis. The innermost ring is dashed and labelled 'Google's 5% CTR floor', so policy literacy is visible before the first scroll. Three real outcomes (COPD Foundation 300+ screenings/month, UNCF $38,000/month in donations, Solar Sister +3,000% search traffic) are pinned exactly where a ray meets an arc, each with vertical, twelve-month period and a case-file link, so a board-forwarding ED reads a record, not a promise. Real sound attenuates; on this site the signal grows, which is precisely NPM's claim, and every chip proves it with a date.

The diagram is declared in two layers. The geometry (rays, arcs, bars, trace, origin ring, ruled grid) is decorative and lives in one aria-hidden inline SVG sized to its grid column. Everything that carries information (the floor label, the lead chip, the three outcome chips, the axis label) is real DOM text, and a visually hidden figcaption states the whole argument in one sentence. That split is also what lets the hero degrade: when the hero container drops below 960px the arc field is swapped for the stacked level bar, the same component the phone hero uses, and the h1 scales with container units so it always holds two lines. Motion is pure CSS (arcs grow from the origin, bars rise, chips fade up, 1.4s total) and collapses to the final static state under prefers-reduced-motion; no JavaScript is required for any of it.

The panel's sharpest critiques were that trust was carried by geometry rather than evidence and that the field had no human in it. Both are fixed at the source: the account lead sits at the origin as a person, Amber Snow's portrait and 'Our guarantee is a person, not a policy' sit under the primary CTA, and the trust ledger is a framed Letter from Google (Michelle Hurtado at 21px serif), a status ticker with an 'as of' date, and a typographic client roll set in five voices between hairlines like a donor wall. The register stays confident calm with a pulse: warm paper, near-black ink, one vermilion for arcs and the single emphasised headline word, a darker vermilion for buttons and small labels so every piece of text clears AA, and the mark's blue kept exactly where the brand lives: the logo tile, the origin dot, the Google stamp and the focus ring.

Risks

  • Red carries a second meaning: alarm, protest, error. The system depends on discipline (one vermilion word per headline, arcs never filled, max 3px strokes, errors in ink, small vermilion text only in #B7311C); lock the SVG tokens and the Webflow classes so a later editor cannot drift it.
  • Abstract geometry can still read as fintech or audio hardware if the human counterweights are cut. The named lead at the origin, Amber Snow under the CTA, the signed monthly note and black-and-white team photography are mandatory, not decorative.
  • Warm paper plus vermilion is a harder internal sell than blue. The mark tile, the blue origin dot, the Google stamp and the contained 'One of nine' band keep #0A23A2 visibly present, but 780 existing clients will notice the change and the wordmark lockup needs redrawing.
  • The bars and arcs encode order, not scale; the figcaptions say so, but a skeptical reader could still read '+3,000%' as a promise. Every figure ships with unit, period and a case-file link, and the sub-copy stays on reach and compliance rather than fundraising ROI.
  • Four Google font families (Bricolage Grotesque variable, Hanken Grotesk, Geist Mono, Instrument Serif) on Webflow need a font-loading budget and subsetting; drop Instrument Serif before dropping anything else.
  • The chips, ledger, checker and price range require NPM to commit to public numbers, periods and policy language; a 'no' on pricing or on named leads degrades the direction and should be answered before build.

The idea

The buyer is a tired executive director or communications lead who screens vendors for transparency and has to defend a flat monthly fee as overhead to a board. Every competitor answers that person with saturated blue, a logo carousel and a #1 claim. Broadsheet answers with the visual grammar of a respected daily: an ear strip with a real dateline and an edition marker, a masthead under an Oxford rule, a seven-and-five column split with a hairline between, small-caps section labels, tabular numerals, dated figures and sourced footnotes. Numbers are printed the way a paper of record prints them, not the way a SaaS stat card shouts them. The emotional register is calm authority: relief, not excitement.

The ground is cool newsprint, not cream. The page is #FAFAF8, the ledger card is pure white, the ink is #111 and the rules are cool gray. The only chromatic colour anywhere is the brand's deep blue #0A23A2, used as a newspaper uses its one spot colour: the CTA, links, the single italic emphasis in the headline, the 'On the record' ledger header, the Google 5% floor line, the CEO's signature and a duotone portrait plate. There is no red, coral, vermilion, ochre or yellow on the site. That single discipline is what separates it from Amplitude (warm paper, vermilion) and Signed Sentence (oat, forest, ochre), and it makes the rebrand read to 780 existing clients as a sharpening of the blue they already know rather than a rupture.

It fits Nonprofit Megaphone because the company's real assets are documentary, not decorative: 54 case studies, a 5,000-word ungated Ad Grants guide, 618 blog posts, a 60-person named team, an endorsement from Google's first Head of Ad Grants, an inaugural-cohort certification held by nine agencies worldwide, and $48M of managed media. That is a publisher's inventory. The site treats it as one: a ledger with as-of dates, a CTR chart with Google's deactivation floor drawn in, a typographic client roll that reads as a subscriber list, and a human row where Google's endorsement sits on one side of a hairline and Amber Snow's signed guarantee sits on the other. Nothing is a costume: the dateline is the real CMS 'last reviewed' date, the edition marker is the real 'figures updated' month, and the certification line in the ear is a fact.

The idea

The other directions ask the reader to trust a document: a ledger, a broadsheet, a signed note, a record card. Placard asks them to trust a sign. It borrows the lineage of the WPA, Swiss and Bauhaus civic poster, where a public message had to be read from across a street: two flat fields, one object, one line, one rule. The hero is a poster pinned to a white wall. A deep teal field carries the whole promise in three ruled lines, '$10,000 a month. Kept, not lost. Be heard.', the offer, the promise and the mission in the order a placard is read. A mustard field carries the megaphone rebuilt as pure geometry: an ink circle, a teal wedge with an ink rim, and three tomato bars radiating from the mouth through a single halftone disc. Google's 5% rule sits on the poster itself as a tomato badge under the words 'Google's rule', because a rule you can read from across the room is a rule you stop being afraid of.

Everything that proves the claim is set as poster copy: short, loud, ruled. Below the poster, a white imprint strip inside 4px ink rules carries the three figures in numbered circles (100% acquisition guarantee, 780+ nonprofit clients, $48M+ managed), Michelle Hurtado's endorsement under a teal kicker giving her Google title, and the eight client names as tracked capitals between mustard squares. There are no gradients, no shadows, no rounded cards and no photography in the hero; the only curve on the page is a circle. Type does the rest: Archivo Black uppercase at 50 and 88px against Archivo body at 17 and 12px, so the scale jump itself reads as volume. The register is optimistic civic confidence, the tone of a public-works poster rather than a marketing site, which is exactly the calm, forwardable authority a tired executive director wants to put in front of a board.
